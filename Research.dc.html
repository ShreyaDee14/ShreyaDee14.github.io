Research

Dec 2024 — present

Ship trajectory uncertainty & bridge collision risk

Graduate Researcher, Johns Hopkins University
Civil & Systems Engineering
Advisor: Prof. Michael Shields

For my master's thesis, I am privileged to be able to work on an extension of the Bridge Risk JHU project which was initiated following the collision of cargo vessel Dali with the Francis Scott Key Bridge leading to its collapse over the Patapsco River in Maryland, US. I am tasked with modeling ship tracks using Constrained Gaussian Processes Machine Learning under the guidance of Principal Investigator Professor Michael Shields, head of the Shields Uncertainty Research Group (SURG) at Johns Hopkins University. The aim is to study navigation patterns to understand ship aberrancy which further sheds light on enhancing infrastructure safety.

This project is based on an award from the National Science Foundation (NSF): #2428805
RAPID: Assessing Bridge Collapse Risk – Learning from the Francis Scott Key Bridge Collapse, Baltimore, Maryland, March 2024.

Oct 2025 — Dec 2025

Renewable capacity across the EU-27

Graduate Researcher, Johns Hopkins University
Advisors: Daniel Kammen (JHU); Mariusz Baranowski, Piotr Jabkowski (Adam Mickiewicz University)

I am collaborating with Professors Daniel Kammen (Johns Hopkins University), Mariusz Baranowski, and Piotr Jabkowski (Adam Mickiewicz University) on modeling the distribution of renewable energy capacity across EU-27 member states to achieve 2030/2050 climate targets. Building on their deterministic allocation framework, I am developing Monte Carlo simulations to incorporate uncertainty from policy shifts, technological innovation, and economic constraints. This work aims to generate probabilistic deployment scenarios and confidence intervals for country-level targets, while identifying robust allocation strategies and key drivers of variability across the European Union.
